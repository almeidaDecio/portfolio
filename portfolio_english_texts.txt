CASE STUDY — Softfocus Zoom Rural
====================================================================

Section: AgTech FinTech: Optimizing Agricultural Lending Operations

How we redesigned satellite-driven remote monitoring, transforming
expensive field inspections into a scalable compliance platform for
Tier-1 financial institutions.

Executive Summary:

Zoom Rural is an enterprise B2B SaaS platform that enables financial
institutions to monitor, audit, and authorize high-value agricultural
loans. By consolidating satellite imagery, weather intelligence, tax
invoices, and environmental data, the platform mitigates operational
risks and ensures compliance with strict national regulatory standards.

As the Lead Product Designer on this product for 3 years, I spearheaded
the redesign of the remote monitoring dashboard. The project transformed
a dense, fragmented data interface into an intuitive, high-scannability
product. This evolution directly enabled our business to acquire Tier-1
financial giants, including Santander and Banco do Brasil.

Key Metrics: 500+ financial branches · BRL 2B+ in annual transactions
· 100K+ rural producers impacted

The Challenge:

In agricultural lending, financial institutions must verify that
approved credit is used exactly as declared by the farmer. Historically,
this required expensive, unscalable in-person field inspections — forcing
field agents to physically travel to remote farms to generate reports.

To automate this, Zoom Rural integrated remote sensing, satellite
imaging, and agrometeorological databases. However, as the product grew,
we faced a major UX bottleneck:
- Data Overload: The system began pulling massive data layers — including
  climatic risk models (ZARC), weather events, crop growth phases, and
  financial invoice validations.
- Poor Hierarchy: The UI stacked all this information vertically. Users
  had to scroll endlessly through massive pages, losing visual context
  of the satellite map.
- Conflicting Personas: Different users had conflicting goals. Risk
  Analysts needed quick, macro-level status summaries, while Field
  Technicians needed to perform deep technical audits.

My Design & Research Strategy:

1. User Discovery & Persona Alignment:
I conducted deep user interviews with both Agro-Credit Analysts and
Technical Agronomists at major lending cooperatives. This research
revealed a critical divergence in information priorities:
- The Analyst wanted an immediate 'Healthy / At Risk' verdict on the
  farm's status to accelerate credit approvals.
- The Technician needed to verify specific environmental and regulatory
  details, drill down into crop growth phases, and cross-reference
  weather anomalies against satellite timelines.

2. Information Architecture: The Nested Navigation Solution:
The central challenge was presenting high-density data on a single page
alongside a dynamic satellite map without overwhelming the user. I
proposed a structured, tabbed layout system:
- The Primary Canvas: Kept the high-resolution satellite imagery front
  and center, displaying key crop health indicators.
- Contextual Data Panes: Grouped data into four highly targeted tabs —
  Agrometeorological Risk Zoning (ZARC), Crop Cycle Analysis, Weather
  Anomalies, and Analytics.

This design reduced the page height by 70%, allowing users to access
any data point with a single, horizontal click while keeping the
satellite map perfectly in view.

3. Redesigning the Compliance Reports:
Enterprise users frequently need to download and present audit reports
to compliance boards. I audited competitive products and restructured
our reporting engine:
- Full Comprehensive Report: Streamlined the layout to align with the
  new desktop visual hierarchy, ensuring regulatory auditability.
- The One-Page Executive Summary (Zero-to-One): Designed a brand-new,
  ultra-condensed brief summarizing the entire operation on a single
  page — a massive competitive differentiator praised by credit managers.

Business Impact & Outcomes:
- Tier-1 Client Acquisition: Helped Softfocus secure major enterprise
  accounts, including Santander and Banco do Brasil (the largest
  agricultural lender in Latin America).
- Increased Product Adoption: Drove significant growth in daily active
  usage among existing cooperative clients.
- Zero-to-One Competitive Edge: The simplified executive report became
  a key differentiator in product demos and corporate pitches.

CASE STUDY — Gamegather
====================================================================

Section: Gamegather: Designing an Anti-Toxicity Gaming Community

How we architected a mobile-first social platform that matches players
based on behavioral compatibility, style of play, and peer-vetted
reputation.

Executive Summary:

Gamegather is a mobile-first social networking platform built to connect
online gamers based on playstyle, interests, and behavior. The platform
was designed to combat online toxicity and help casual and competitive
players easily discover safe, engaging multiplayer communities.

As a Contract Product Designer, I spent 6 months driving the core UX
architecture, user research, navigation models, and responsive interface
design.

The Challenge:

In modern multiplayer gaming, players face two massive friction points:
- Extreme Toxicity: Standard matchmaking algorithms only group players
  by skill level, completely ignoring behavioral alignment. This exposes
  users to verbal abuse and toxic behavior.
- In-App Fragmentation: Gamers rely on a chaotic mix of Discord servers,
  subreddits, and third-party apps just to find a reliable team.

Our challenge was to design a unified, mobile-first experience that
lowered the friction of finding compatible players while building
active, self-policing community loops.

My Design Process:

1. Competitive Benchmarking:
I analyzed leading community platforms, social apps, and gaming tools —
including Discord, Tinder, and esports trackers. The key discovery was
that while existing apps excel at messaging and grouping, they fail at
trust and behavior validation. This created a huge opportunity to
position Gamegather around a behavioral reputation system.

2. User Research & Behavioral Architecture:
I conducted interviews with casual and competitive gamers to understand
how they evaluate trust online. We identified that 'good gaming' isn't
just about winning — it's about communication style, casualness vs.
competitiveness, and respect.

From this, I designed:
- Player Profile Cards: Focused on playstyle tags (e.g., 'Chill,'
  'Tactical,' 'No Mic Needed') rather than just rank.
- Reputation Loop: A post-match endorsement system that allows teammates
  to award badges for positive behavior — preventing text-based
  moderation issues.

3. Mobile-First Navigation:
Given that gamers use social apps on their phones while sitting at their
PCs or consoles, a streamlined, single-handed mobile navigation was
essential. I designed a bottom-bar-centric layout with quick access to:
- Discover Hub: Tailored player recommendations.
- Lobbies: Active groups seeking players.
- Inbox & Messages: Instant communication.

Design & Visual Direction:

The interface utilizes a sleek dark-mode aesthetic with vibrant,
high-contrast neon accents, capturing the modern energy of gaming
culture without compromising on accessibility. The typography and card
systems were structured for extremely fast scanning, allowing users to
evaluate a group's compatibility in under three seconds.

CASE STUDY — Secret Santa (Amigo Secreto)
====================================================================

Section: Secret Santa for Teams: Gamifying Corporate Holiday Celebrations

How I designed, prototyped, and validated a mobile-first gift-exchange
app with interactive gamification in a high-intensity, one-week design
sprint.

Context & The Brief:

As part of the hiring process for Softfocus, I was given a one-week open
design challenge: create a digital solution to streamline corporate
Secret Santa gift exchanges. The prompt addressed a real operational
headache — as corporate teams grow and transition to remote/hybrid
setups, holiday gift exchanges become chaotic.

The brief required: automated drawing mechanics, interactive gift
registries, participant profile tracking, mechanics to boost the chance
of drawing a specific person, and crowdsourced proposals. Bonus goal:
gamification elements — daily quizzes, unlockable hints, and
achievements.

The Challenge:

- Automated drawing mechanics
- Interactive gift registries with store links
- Draw details and participant profile tracking
- Mechanics to strategically boost the chance of drawing a specific person
- Crowdsourced proposals (e.g., voting to adjust the budget)
- Gamification: daily quizzes, unlockable hints, achievements

My Design Process:

1. Functional Mapping & Architecture:
To succeed in a high-velocity sprint, I first mapped the requirements
into discrete functional modules: matchmaking (automated draw logic with
customizable exclusion rules), registry (a visual catalog for store links
and price ranges), interaction (private in-app messaging), and
gamification (daily micro-challenges to unlock clues).

2. Bifurcated User Journeys:
- The Organizer: Needs seamless group creation, invitation management,
  rule configuration, and automated draw execution.
- The Participant: Demands frictionless entry, rapid registry setup,
  private chatting, and quick access to gamified activities.

3. Competitor Benchmarking:
I reviewed five global holiday-drawing apps. The research revealed that
while competitors managed drawing mechanics well, they lacked ongoing
user engagement — participants downloaded the app, drew a name, and
immediately uninstalled it. This validated my decision to focus heavily
on in-app messaging and gamification to drive continuous daily retention.

4. Brand & UI Design System:
- Visual Metaphor: Interfaces inspired by layered wrapping paper, bows,
  and holiday patterns.
- Color Palette: Premium Deep Slate (#1D3557), Warm Teal (#457B9D),
  Crimson Red (#E63946), and Winter Mint (#A8DADC).
- Typography: Proza Libre for warm, playful headings and Open Sans for
  highly readable body copy.

5. Prototyping & Usability Testing:
I built a fully functional high-fidelity prototype in Figma. On day 6,
I ran rapid usability tests with five users. Group creation and the
gamified daily quiz were highly intuitive. The gift registry flow was
initially confusing — I quickly redesigned the screen to place
user-added wishlist items above automated store recommendations.

Retrospective & Strategic Reflection:

This high-fidelity project was highly successful, serving as the primary
baseline that led to my recruitment and subsequent five-year career at
Softfocus.

Looking back with years of B2B SaaS experience, I identify two key areas
for improvement:
- Accessibility (WCAG Compliance): The teal color (#457B9D) against a
  white background falls below the WCAG AA contrast ratio of 4.5:1.
- Product Refinement: I would streamline the product registry layout to
  utilize a more compact card system for smaller mobile screens.

CASE STUDY — Hurst Capital
====================================================================

Section: Hurst Capital: Redesigning the Alternative Asset Investment
Experience

How I redesigned the home and editorial pages for a leading financial
platform, balancing high-density investment data with premium visual
trust.

Context & The Brief:

Hurst Capital is Latin America's pioneer in alternative asset
investments, offering access to high-yield, non-traditional assets like
private equity, real estate, litigation funding, and intellectual
property.

As part of a professional UX/UI evaluation challenge, I was tasked with
redesigning two core touchpoints: the Home Page and the Editorial Blog.
The primary goal was to modernize the aesthetic, establish a rigorous
visual hierarchy, and build deep credibility without alienating the
platform's sophisticated investor base.

The Challenge:

The existing site suffered from severe visual hierarchy challenges:
- High Cognitive Load: Extremely long, dense blocks of text with no
  visual breathing room.
- Underwhelming Visuals: Small, low-impact imagery that failed to
  capture the institutional magnitude of alternative assets.
- Poor Navigation: A rigid header menu that failed to provide clear
  orientation or structural context.

My Redesign Process:

1. Market Benchmarking:
I analyzed premium international investment platforms — Stripe,
Wealthfront, and premium asset management sites — noting how they
handled generous negative space, strong structured typographic systems,
and clean cards-based layouts.

2. Key Design System Decisions:
- Responsive Grid: Implemented a rigid 12-column Bootstrap grid system.
- Negative Space & Contrast: Introduced generous margins between content
  blocks.
- Typographic Hierarchy: Dramatically increased the scale of hero
  headers.
- Global Navigation Anchors: Redesigned the navigation bar with a
  prominent active-state indicator.

3. Execution & Touchpoints:

The Home Page Redesign:
I transformed the hero section with an impactful, high-resolution
background asset, a powerful left-aligned headline, and a prominent CTA.
Subsequent blocks were divided into clean, alternating content
containers.

The Blog & Editorial Redesign:
The original blog listed financial articles in a dense, uninviting
vertical list. I redesigned it into a modern, responsive card-based grid.
Each card dynamically displays the category tag, editorial image,
read-time, and bold title, enabling investors to find relevant analysis
in seconds.

Retrospective & Strategic Refinement:

The redesign was highly successful, generating a direct invitation to
final-round interviews. Critically evaluating this project today, I
believe the team card module could be streamlined further — using a
modal system to display full professional bios while keeping the primary
layout ultra-clean.
